Week Beginner Community Program
Digital Realm Entertainment  

## Program Overview

Program Name: DRE Kigali Game Builders Bootcamp 
Duration: 4 weeks (February 10 - March 9, 2026)  
Target Audience: Beginner game developers and aspiring e-sports players in Kigali and East Africa  
Format: Hybrid (in-person at Norrsken + online via Discord)

### Objectives
1. Community Growth: Onboard 50+ new active members to DRE community
2. Skill Development: Equip beginners with foundational game dev and e-sports skills
3. Engagement: Create 4 touchpoint events that build lasting connections
4. Pipeline: Identify 10-15 participants for advanced programs (Game Jams, tournaments)
5. Retention: Achieve 70% attendance rate across all sessions

## Weekly Session Breakdown

Week 1: Welcome to The Realm (Feb 10-14)
  Theme: Introduction & Fundamentals  
  Session: "Game Dev 101: Your First Steps"

  Objectives:
    - Orient newcomers to DRE ecosystem and tools
    - Teach basic game design concepts
    - Build initial community bonds

  Activities:
    - Opening Circle (30 min): Icebreakers, program overview, expectations
    - Workshop (90 min): Introduction to Unity/Godot basics, download setup assistance
    - Show & Tell (30 min): DRE team presents past GGJ projects for inspiration
    - Networking (30 min): Informal snacks and Discord community setup

  Deliverables: Participants create a simple game  
  Venue: Norrsken East Africa, Kigali (or online via Discord for remote participants)  
  Target Attendance: 60 people

Week 2: Build Your First Game (Feb 17-21)
  Theme: Hands-On Creation  
  Session: "Game Jam Lite: 2-Hour Sprint"

  Objectives:
    - Apply Week 1 concepts in a low-pressure creative environment
    - Introduce collaborative game development
    - Spark creativity and experimentation

  Activities:
    - Mini Game Jam (2 hrs): Teams of 2-3 build a mini-game around a simple theme 
    - Playtesting Circle (45 min): Teams demo their games and give/receive feedback
    - Beginner E-sports Showcase (30 min): Local player demonstrates gameplay techniques
    - Q&A Panel (15 min): DRE developers answer questions

  Deliverables: Each team submits a playable prototype  
  Venue: Norrsken East Africa / Discord  
  Target Attendance: 45 people


 Week 3: Level Up Your Skills (Feb 24-28)
    Theme: Intermediate Techniques  
    Session: Polish & Publish Your Game

    Objectives:
      - Teach basic art, sound, and UI/UX for games
      - Introduce itch.io and publishing platforms
      - Connect participants to Pixel Realm asset marketplace

    Activities:
      - Workshop Track A - Game Dev (90 min): Adding polish (sound effects, UI, basic animations using free assets)
      - Workshop Track B - E-sports (90 min): Competitive gameplay fundamentals, tournament prep basics
      - Lightning Talks (30 min): 3x10-min talks from community members on their projects
      - Open Studio Time (30 min): Work on projects with mentor support

    Deliverables: Improved game builds OR recorded gameplay montage for e-sports track  
    Venue: Norrsken East Africa / Discord  
    Target Attendance: 30 people


Week 4: Showcase & Next Steps (Mar 3-7)
    Theme: Celebration & Continuation  
    Session: Demo Day & The Path Forward

    Objectives:
      - Celebrate participant achievements
      - Introduce advanced DRE programs (GGJ, NCG tournaments)
      - Gather feedback and retain engagement

    Activities:
      - Demo Day (60 min): Participants present their games/montages to the community
      - Awards Ceremony (20 min): Prizes for Most Creative, Best Teamwork, People's Choice
      - Roadmap Session (30 min): Overview of GGJ 2027, NCG tournaments, WPGA events
      - Feedback & Networking (30 min): Program survey + informal celebration

    Deliverables: Published game on itch.io OR tournament signup  
    Venue: Norrsken East Africa (hybrid streaming via Discord)  
    Target Attendance: 35 people

Risk Mitigation (High-Level)

1. Low turnout: Over-recruit by 20%, create FOMO through early bird perks
2. Technical issues: Pre-test all equipment, have backup offline activities
3. Participant skill gaps: Pair beginners with slightly more experienced peers
4. Budget overruns: Lock in vendor quotes, maintain 15% contingency fund
5. Low engagement: Gamify participation (points for attendance, demos, feedback)
