## Risk #1: Low Participant Turnout

### Description
Fewer than 30 participants attend sessions (target: 40+), leading to weak community energy, underutilized resources, and poor ROI.

### Root Causes
- Ineffective marketing (low reach, unclear value proposition)
- Registration-to-attendance drop-off (people register but don't show up)
- Competing events or commitments (exams, work, other tech meetups)
- Venue accessibility issues (transportation, parking, safety concerns)

### Impact if Realized
- **High:** Insufficient critical mass for peer learning and networking; budget wasted on refreshments/materials; team morale dip; negative perception of DRE programs.

### Likelihood: Medium (2)
- Digital Realm Entertainment has existing community base, but this is a new program format.
- Beginner-focused programs often see 30-40% no-show rates.

### **Mitigation Strategies (Pre-Event)**

| Strategy | Action | Owner | Deadline |
|----------|--------|-------|----------|
| **Over-recruit by 30%** | Target 60-70 registrations for 40-50 expected attendees | Community Manager | Feb 5 |
| **Multi-channel marketing** | Social media + email + campus ambassadors + partner networks | Marketing Lead | Feb 3 |
| **Clear value proposition** | Emphasize "free," "beginner-friendly," "publishable game in 4 weeks," "no experience required" in all comms | Content Creator | Feb 1 |
| **Multiple reminders** | Send 3 emails: confirmation (T-7 days), reminder (T-2 days), day-of (morning) | Community Manager | Ongoing |
| **Waitlist management** | Convert no-shows into waitlist opportunities; SMS reminders for confirmed attendees | Registration Coordinator | Feb 10 |
| **Venue accessibility** | Share clear directions, parking info, public transport options; offer hybrid option for remote folks | Event Lead | Feb 8 |

### **Contingency Plan (If Turnout < 20)**
1. **Proceed with event:** Smaller group = more personalized support, deeper relationships.
2. **Adjust format:** Shift from lecture-style to workshop circles (intimate, interactive).
3. **Analyze drop-off:** Survey no-shows ("What prevented you from attending?") to fix for Week 2.
4. **Intensify outreach:** Launch urgent social media push and personal outreach to registrants for Week 2.
5. **Reduce costs:** Scale back refreshments, skip printing extras, reallocate budget to paid ads for Week 2.

### Success Indicator
- ✅ 35+ attendees at Week 1 (target: 40+)
- ✅ 70%+ registration-to-attendance conversion rate



## Risk #2: Technical Infrastructure Failures

### Description
WiFi outages, projector malfunctions, livestream crashes, or participant laptop issues derail the workshop, frustrate attendees, and damage DRE's credibility.

### Root Causes
- Unreliable venue WiFi (Norrsken infrastructure issues)
- AV equipment failure (projector, microphones, adapters)
- Livestream platform glitches (Discord, StreamYard)
- Participants arrive with incompatible devices (Chromebooks, tablets, old laptops)
- Power outages 

### Impact if Realized
- **High:** Workshop cannot proceed as planned; participants leave frustrated; online audience disengages; reputation damage ("DRE can't even run a basic event").

### Likelihood: Medium (2)
- WiFi/power issues common in Kigali; AV tech can be finicky; first-time event = untested systems.

### **Mitigation Strategies (Pre-Event)**

| Strategy | Action | Owner | Deadline |
|----------|--------|-------|----------|
| **Venue tech walkthrough** | Test WiFi speed, projector, microphones, power outlets 2 days before each event | Tech Lead | T-2 days |
| **Backup internet** | Bring mobile hotspot (MTN/Airtel data plan) as fallback | Tech Support | Feb 10 |
| **Offline contingency materials** | Pre-load Unity/Godot tutorials on USB drives; download backup slides/videos | Workshop Facilitator | Feb 8 |
| **Equipment redundancy** | Bring spare HDMI cables, adapters (USB-C, VGA), laptop chargers, power strips | Tech Support | T-1 day |
| **Pre-event participant prep** | Email attendees with pre-work: install Unity/Godot, download required assets, charge laptops | Community Manager | T-5 days |
| **Livestream dry run** | Test Discord Go Live or StreamYard setup 24 hours before event | Tech Support | T-1 day |
| **Generator access** | Confirm Norrsken has backup generator; know location of manual restart | Venue Liaison | Feb 1 |

### **Contingency Plan (If Tech Fails)**

| Scenario | Response |
|----------|----------|
| **WiFi down** | Switch to offline activities: pre-downloaded tutorial videos, USB-based asset sharing, whiteboard coding exercises |
| **Projector fails** | Facilitator shares screen via participant laptops (small group clusters); use printed handouts |
| **Livestream crashes** | Apologize to online attendees, record session for later upload to YouTube/Discord, prioritize in-person experience |
| **Power outage** | Move to battery-powered laptops for small-group work; reschedule live demo for later if needed |
| **Participant laptop issues** | Pair affected participants with peers for screen-sharing; have 2-3 backup laptops available (DRE team devices) |

### Success Indicator
- ✅ 95%+ uptime for core infrastructure (WiFi, projector, livestream) across all 4 weeks
- ✅ Zero event cancellations or major delays due to technical issues



## Risk #3: Participant Skill Gaps Too Wide

### Description
Some participants are complete beginners while others have intermediate experience, making it difficult to pace workshops effectively. Advanced participants get bored; novices get overwhelmed.

### Root Causes
- Registration form doesn't adequately screen for skill level
- Marketing attracts mixed audience (hobbyists, students, professionals)
- Self-assessed skill levels inaccurate (beginner vs. "I've watched YouTube tutorials")
- No pre-event skill leveling (everyone starts Week 1 at same point)

### Impact if Realized
- **Medium:** Frustrated beginners drop out; advanced participants disengage; facilitator struggles to balance pacing; negative feedback on surveys.

### Likelihood: High (3)
- Almost guaranteed in beginner-focused programs; hard to control who registers.

### **Mitigation Strategies (Pre-Event)**

| Strategy | Action | Owner | Deadline |
|----------|--------|-------|----------|
| **Skill-based registration** | Add question to Microsoft Form: "Rate your game dev experience (1-5)" with examples for each level | Registration Coordinator | Feb 1 |
| **Explicit beginner messaging** | Marketing emphasizes "designed for total beginners" to set expectations | Marketing Lead | Feb 3 |
| **Pre-work for all levels** | Beginners: Install Unity/Godot. Intermediates: Review specific tutorial to calibrate expectations | Community Manager | Feb 7 |
| **Peer mentorship model** | Pair advanced participants with beginners (publicly frame as "leadership opportunity") | Community Host | Week 1 |
| **Breakout tracks (Week 2+)** | Offer "Beginner" and "Intermediate" parallel workshops if skill gap persists | Workshop Facilitator | Week 2 planning |
| **Stretch challenges** | Prepare bonus tasks for fast learners (e.g., "Add a scoring system," "Implement AI enemy") | Workshop Facilitator | Feb 8 |

### **Contingency Plan (If Gap Too Wide)**
1. **Real-time adjustment:** Facilitator slows down if majority struggling; Community Host circulates to help stuck participants.
2. **Leverage advanced participants:** Ask intermediate folks to mentor 1-2 beginners (gamify it with "Mentor MVP" recognition).
3. **Split the room (if 10+ intermediates):** Create beginner and intermediate clusters with separate facilitators (use DRE team member for second track).
4. **Async support:** Post tutorial videos in Discord for slower learners to catch up at their own pace.
5. **Post-Week 1 survey:** Ask "Was the pace too fast, too slow, or just right?" Adjust Week 2+ accordingly.

### Success Indicator
- ✅ 70%+ of participants rate pacing as "just right" on feedback surveys
- ✅ Retention rate of 70%+ from Week 1 → Week 4 (no mass drop-offs due to skill mismatch)


## Risk #4: Budget Overruns

### Description
Actual spending exceeds planned budget ($530), forcing last-minute cost-cutting or requiring additional funding approval, which delays operations.

### Root Causes
- Underestimated costs (refreshments for more attendees than expected)
- Unexpected expenses (equipment rental, printing overages, emergency supplies)
- Prize budget creep (pressure to offer bigger rewards to drive engagement)
- Vendor price increases (inflation, last-minute orders)

### Impact if Realized
- **Medium:** Cash flow issues; need to reallocate from other DRE budgets; reduced refreshments/swag quality; team stress.

### Likelihood: Low (1)
- Budget is conservative ($530); built-in cost-saving strategies; DRE has financial oversight.

### **Mitigation Strategies (Pre-Event)**

| Strategy | Action | Owner | Deadline |
|----------|--------|-------|----------|
| **Lock in vendor quotes** | Get written quotes for refreshments, printing by Feb 3; pre-pay if possible to avoid price hikes | Event Lead | Feb 3 |
| **15% contingency buffer** | Earmark $80 (~15% of $530) as "emergency fund" for unplanned costs | Finance Liaison | Feb 1 |
| **Cost-per-attendee caps** | Refreshments: max $5/person; printing: max $1.50/person | Event Lead | Feb 1 |
| **In-kind partnerships** | Seek donated refreshments (local bakery, beverage sponsor), free swag (DRE stickers, Pixel Realm credits) | Partnerships Lead | Feb 1 |
| **Weekly expense tracking** | Log all spending in shared Google Sheet; flag if approaching 80% of weekly budget | Event Lead | Weekly |
| **Low-cost alternatives** | Prizes: DRE swag, free NCG tournament entry, Pixel Realm asset credits (zero cash cost) | Community Manager | Feb 1 |

### **Contingency Plan (If Budget Exceeds $600)**
1. **Immediate cost audit:** Identify which category exceeded (refreshments, printing, etc.).
2. **Scale back non-essentials:** Cut Week 4 decorations, reduce swag quantity, use digital certificates instead of printed.
3. **Reallocate internally:** Request $100-200 from DRE marketing or events budget (pre-approved authority from leadership).
4. **Negotiate with vendors:** Ask for last-minute discounts, deferred payment, or in-kind trade (e.g., social media shoutouts).
5. **Fundraise from participants:** Optional "suggested donation" of $2-5 for refreshments (not mandatory, to keep program free).

### Success Indicator
- ✅ Total spending stays within $530 ± 10% ($477-583)
- ✅ Zero unplanned funding requests to DRE leadership



## Risk #5: Low Post-Program Retention

### Description
Participants complete the 4 weeks but disengage immediately after, failing to join advanced programs (GGJ 2027, NCG tournaments) or remain active in Discord community.

### Root Causes
- No clear next steps communicated at Demo Day
- Participants feel they've "graduated" and move on
- Lack of ongoing value (no reason to stay engaged)
- Competing priorities (work, school) after program ends
- Community fatigue (4 weeks is intense, people need a break)

### Impact if Realized
- **High:** Low conversion to advanced programs (defeats pipeline goal); Discord community becomes inactive; wasted effort building relationships; poor lifetime value of participants.

### Likelihood: Medium (2)
- Common issue in time-bound cohort programs; requires intentional retention design.

### **Mitigation Strategies (During Program)**

| Strategy | Action | Owner | Deadline |
|----------|--------|-------|----------|
| **Early pipeline seeding** | Mention GGJ 2027, NCG tournaments, and monthly Office Hours starting Week 1 | Community Host | Week 1 |
| **Alumni benefits framing** | Create "Realm Starter Alumni" tier with perks: priority GGJ spots, free workshops, mentor opportunities | Community Manager | Week 3 |
| **Post-program roadmap** | At Demo Day, present clear next steps: (1) Monthly Office Hours, (2) GGJ 2027 app opens April 1, (3) Alumni-only game jam in May | Event Lead | Week 4 |
| **Ongoing touchpoints** | Schedule 2 post-program events: Week 6 "Alumni Meetup," Week 10 "Portfolio Review Session" | Community Manager | Week 4 |
| **Discord engagement plan** | Launch async challenges: "Game of the Month," "Code Review Fridays," "Screenshot Saturday" | Discord Moderator | Week 5 |
| **Participant success tracking** | Collect participant emails, Discord handles, itch.io profiles for ongoing comms | Registration Coordinator | Week 1 |

### **Mitigation Strategies (Post-Program)**

| Strategy | Action | Owner | Deadline |
|----------|--------|-------|----------|
| **Thank-you email campaign** | Send personalized thank-you emails within 24 hours of Demo Day with certificates, highlights, next steps | Community Manager | Mar 7 |
| **Weekly newsletter** | Launch "Realm Insider" email (weekly game dev tips, community highlights, event announcements) | Content Creator | Mar 10 |
| **1:1 follow-ups** | Event Lead personally messages top 10 participants to discuss advanced opportunities (mentorship, speaking, GGJ organizing) | Event Lead | Mar 10 |
| **Public celebration** | Feature participant games on DRE website, RGN blog, social media to maintain pride/connection | Marketing Lead | Mar 15 |
| **Feedback survey** | Send survey with question: "What would keep you engaged with DRE after this program?" Use insights to design retention tactics | Community Manager | Mar 7 |

### **Contingency Plan (If Retention < 30%)**
1. **Analyze drop-off:** Survey non-engaged alumni to understand why (too busy, no clear value, preferred other communities).
2. **Reduce friction:** Make engagement easier (e.g., async Discord challenges vs. live events, monthly vs. weekly commitments).
3. **Offer incentives:** Free entry to next paid workshop, exclusive merch, co-branding opportunities for their games.
4. **Retarget via content:** Create valuable content (tutorials, asset packs) delivered via email to keep DRE top-of-mind.
5. **Pivot to lightweight touchpoints:** Shift from expecting active participation to passive community membership (newsletter subscribers, social followers).

### Success Indicator
- ✅ 40%+ of participants join at least one post-program activity (Office Hours, GGJ, alumni meetup) within 3 months
- ✅ 50%+ of participants remain active on Discord (post/react at least once/month) through Month 2


## Risk Monitoring & Escalation Process

### Weekly Risk Review
- **Frequency:** Every Monday during 4-week program
- **Participants:** Event Lead, Community Manager, Workshop Facilitator
- **Agenda:**
  1. Review current risk status (any risks realized or escalating?)
  2. Check mitigation progress (are strategies being executed?)
  3. Flag new risks (did anything unexpected emerge?)
  4. Update risk register if needed

### Escalation Triggers
**Escalate to DRE Leadership if:**
- Any risk score increases to 8-9 (high likelihood + high impact)
- Budget overrun exceeds $200 over planned
- Week 1 attendance < 20 participants (threatens program viability)
- Major technical failure causes event cancellation
- Participant safety/conduct incident occurs

### Risk Owner Responsibilities
1. **Monitor assigned risk** throughout the program
2. **Execute mitigation strategies** on schedule
3. **Report status** at weekly risk reviews
4. **Activate contingency plan** if risk is realized
5. **Document lessons learned** for future cohorts
